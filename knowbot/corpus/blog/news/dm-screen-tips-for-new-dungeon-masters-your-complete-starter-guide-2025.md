## 🛡️ **Tip #1: Start with the Right Screen Setup**

### Choosing Your First DM Screen

Your first screen doesn't need to be complicated, but it should be **functional and expandable**. Avoid the temptation to buy the cheapest cardboard option—you'll outgrow it quickly and end up spending more in the long run.

#### New GM Screen Essentials:

- **Sturdy construction** - Won't tip over during exciting moments
- **Proper height** - Hides your notes but allows eye contact
- **Expandable system** - Can grow with your skills
- **Easy organization** - Attach charts and tools without hassle

**Professional Choice:** The [Ultimate Game Master Screen](https://theultimategamemaster.com/products/ultimate-game-master-screen-brown-portrait) is perfect for new GMs because it grows with you—start simple, add accessories as you gain experience.

### Screen Positioning for Beginners

- **Center yourself at the table** - Equal distance from all players
- **Maintain eye contact** - Players should see your eyes over the screen
- **Leave space behind you** - Room for dice rolling and movement
- **Ensure stability** - Test that nothing wobbles or tips

## ⚔️ **Tip #2: Master Combat Basics First**

### Initiative: Your First Challenge

Combat will make or break your early GMing experience. Nothing kills momentum faster than confused turn order and lost initiative tracking.

#### Simple Initiative System for New GMs:

1. **Roll once, write immediately** - Don't rely on memory
2. **Use visible tracking** - Players need to see turn order
3. **Establish clear routine** - "Sarah, you're up. Tom, you're next."
4. **Keep it moving** - Set a 30-second decision timer

**Game Changer:** [Initiative Trackers](https://theultimategamemaster.com/products/initiative-trackers-brown) eliminate confusion and keep everyone engaged. This single accessory will make you look like a veteran GM from session one.

### Managing Enemies Without Chaos

New GMs often struggle with tracking multiple creatures. Here's the secret: **keep it simple**.

#### Beginner Creature Management:

- **Group similar creatures** - Three goblins = one initiative, shared HP pool
- **Track current HP only** - Not damage taken (stay positive!)
- **Use simple condition markers** - Coins, tokens, or notes
- **Prepare creature cards** - Essential stats on index cards

## 📚 **Tip #3: Organize Information by Importance**

### The New GM Information Hierarchy

#### Must-Have (Always Visible):

- Player character AC and passive Perception
- Current encounter creature stats
- Initiative order for active combat
- Your most commonly used house rules

#### Should-Have (Quick Reference):

- **[Essential rule charts](https://theultimategamemaster.com/products/5th-edition-rules-pack)** - Combat actions, conditions, DCs
- Spell save DC quick reference
- Common environmental hazards
- Social encounter guidelines

#### Nice-to-Have (Nearby Storage):

- Full monster stat blocks
- Detailed spell descriptions
- Campaign background information
- Advanced tactical options

### Common New GM Mistake:

**Don't try to organize everything at once!** Start with the must-haves and add complexity as you become comfortable. Information overload is worse than having too little.

## 🚫 **Tip #5: Avoid These Common New GM Mistakes**

### Screen Setup Mistakes:

- ❌ **Too much information** - Overwhelming yourself with charts
- ❌ **Poor attachment methods** - Using tape that fails during sessions
- ❌ **Wrong screen height** - Too tall creates a barrier with players
- ❌ **Unstable positioning** - Screen that tips or wobbles

### Session Flow Mistakes:

- ❌ **Over-preparing details** - Spending hours on unused content
- ❌ **Under-preparing basics** - Not having essential rules ready
- ❌ **Ignoring player cues** - Missing when players are confused or bored
- ❌ **Perfectionism paralysis** - Stopping the game to find the "right" rule

### Combat Management Mistakes:

- ❌ **Tracking everything individually** - Making simple encounters complex
- ❌ **Forgetting initiative order** - Creating confusion and delays
- ❌ **Overcomplicating conditions** - Using every status effect immediately
- ❌ **Analysis paralysis** - Spending too long on creature decisions

### Helpful Apps for New Game Masters:

#### Essential Apps:

- **D&D Beyond** - Quick spell lookups and rule clarifications
- **Simple dice apps** - When you need unusual dice combinations
- **Timer apps** - Keep turns and sessions moving
- **Note-taking apps** - For session recaps and player achievements

#### Digital Integration Best Practices:

- **Phone positioning** - Use a [proper holder](https://theultimategamemaster.com/products/magnetic-phone-holder-brown) so it doesn't clutter your table
- **Backup everything** - Always have physical copies of critical information
- **Battery management** - Charge devices before every session
- **Minimize distractions** - Turn off notifications during play

### New GM Digital Rule:

**Digital tools should enhance your game, not replace preparation.** Use technology to access information faster, but don't rely on it entirely.

## ⚡ **Tip #7: Develop Your Pre-Session Routine**

### 15-Minute Setup Checklist for New GMs:

1. □ **Screen positioned and stable** (2 minutes)
2. □ **Session notes and charts attached** (3 minutes)
3. □ **Dice and writing supplies organized** (2 minutes)
4. □ **Player character info accessible** (2 minutes)
5. □ **First encounter prepared** (3 minutes)
6. □ **Digital devices ready** (2 minutes)
7. □ **Quick accessibility test** (1 minute)

### Mental Preparation:

- **Review session goals** - What do you want to accomplish?
- **Identify potential challenges** - Where might players surprise you?
- **Practice NPC voices** - Even simple vocal changes help
- **Remind yourself: players want you to succeed** - Everyone's on the same team

## 🎯 **Tip #8: Customize for Your Players**

### Reading Your Table:

#### Combat-Loving Players:

- Focus on [combat management tools](https://theultimategamemaster.com/collections/combat-management)
- Prepare multiple battle maps
- Have creature variety ready

#### Roleplay-Focused Groups:

- Keep mechanics simple initially
- Prepare encouraging feedback
